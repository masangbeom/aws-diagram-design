First to sixth in four quarters, and never a sudden drop

One rank, then two more, then two more. No single quarter looks alarming, which is exactly how a default dies once Graviton is on the table — each straight segment joins two measured ranks and says nothing about the weeks between, so the chart claims the decline happened, not when in each quarter it happened.

m7g took first place in Q2 and the line goes flat

A flat line at rank one is the strongest claim a bump chart makes: nothing displaced it for three consecutive snapshots. Note what it does not say — vCPU-hours could have doubled or halved in that time. Rank hides magnitude; that trade is the price of reading position at a glance.

c7g moved one rank and that is the whole story

Three flat quarters, then a single step up — inherited from m5 falling past it, not from growth of its own. A bump chart cannot tell those apart, which is why the footnote names the ranking key: the reader who needs magnitude follows the measure, not the picture.
